# Paper assets

Three replaceable layers, kept separate on purpose (implementation spec
§2.3, §6): the **mask** defines the shape, the **texture** defines the
material, and the **fringe** defines the depth. Each is reached through one
CSS custom property in `../../components/paper/torn-paper.css`, so any of
them can be swapped without touching `TornPaperSection.tsx`.

| File | Role | Status |
|---|---|---|
| `tear-{a,b,c}-{top,bottom}.svg` | torn silhouette, vector contour | **hand-authored**, retouchable |
| `fringe-{a,b,c}-{top,bottom}.png` | fibre and relief along the cut | generated from the same contour |
| `paper-fibers.webp` | supplied paper artwork | source, untouched |
| `paper-fibers.prepared.webp` | the same, re-centred on mid-grey | derived, generated |

Three contours cover five sheets, so two of them repeat a silhouette. What
must not repeat is the shape AND its phase: `tearOffset` starts a sheet
further into the tiled strip, so the two never show the same lobes in the
same places. Asserted in `PlazaScreen.test.tsx`, which also requires that
no two ADJACENT sheets share a contour at all — their edges meet across
one band of skin.

**A mask and its fringe are a pair.** They are generated from one contour
array and only align together; overriding `--tear-*` without
`--fringe-*` puts the material of one edge on the silhouette of another.
`torn-paper.test.ts` fails the build if a tear variant overrides one
without the other.

## No placeholders left

Both the paper surface and the fringe are real assets now.

### The fringe

Generated by `scripts/author_tears.py` from the SAME contour array as the
mask — the only way the two can stay aligned. A fringe drawn independently
drifts along the edge and reads as a stamped-on strip.

Which marks it draws depends on which side of the tear they land, because
that decides what is visible at all:

- **above the contour** the backdrop is black skin, so only LIGHT marks
  register — the relief, the lit edge of the torn card, and the reason the
  cut reads as thick rather than flat
- **below the contour** the backdrop is coloured paper, so DARK marks
  register — short broken strands overhanging the sheet, plus the
  localized shadow they drop on it

Alpha is zero everywhere else, so it never paints over the continuous
black skin, and the CSS layer gives it no background colour of its own —
asserted in `torn-paper.test.ts`.

Strands are short and sparse on purpose. The first pass ran 1500 of them
averaging 4px and the edge read as fur rather than torn card; torn paper
sheds a little fibre, it does not grow a pelt.

**Where the light goes.** Only the large raised crowns catch it, and they
cluster, so highlights come in patches with dark stretches between — 14
patches on tear-a's top edge, median 3.5 CSS px, median gap 21 CSS px.
This is the whole discipline of the edge, and it has one rule behind it:
**light placed relative to the contour follows the contour**, which is an
outline however finely it is broken up. So light is attached to discrete
fragments, never to the edge itself. Chips stay dark — a chip is a few px
of hang, and a scatter of bright dots along a tear is the outline again.

The same rule governs `--skin-lip-*` in `theme.css`: those bands ARE the
mask offset by 1-5px, so whatever luminance they carry becomes a rim along
the entire tear. They are held under luminance 40 by a test.

The relief lives in one `RELIEF` block in `scripts/author_tears.py` and
knows nothing about the contour or the paper texture, so it can be retuned
or replaced without regenerating geometry.

**On `paper-fibers.prepared.webp`.** `soft-light` is neutral only at
mid-grey, and the supplied artwork means 156. Blended raw it lightens every
sheet and shifts the palette the ink contrast was measured from, so
`scripts/prepare_paper_texture.py` re-centres it on 128 and scales its
deviation. The supplied file stays here untouched as the input; re-run the
script after replacing it.

To swap: drop the file in and change the one `url(...)` in
`torn-paper.css`. Nothing else moves.

## The tear contours

**Hand-authored**, in `scripts/author_tears.py`: a table of points placed
one lobe at a time, not a sum of frequencies. Summing octaves gives a wave
however many you stack — periodic spacing, every crest like its neighbours
— which is why the earlier contour read as decorative rather than torn.

The table carries the macro shape: rounded lobes of varying width, genuine
undercuts where x steps backwards so the black overhangs, secondary tears
on the flanks, and long shallow runs between. A separate fibre pass adds
the 2-3px roughness the reference has along every edge.

Retouch either the table or the emitted path directly. Two contracts, both
asserted by the script:

- x spans exactly 0..800 and the first and last `y` are **equal**, or the
  tile shows a seam under `mask-repeat: repeat-x`
- `y` stays inside [16, 98], so the tear never bites past the strip

Tiled rather than stretched, so a tall section never elongates the
contour (§8).
